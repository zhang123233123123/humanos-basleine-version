# Event segmentation audit v0.1

## Purpose and boundary

This document records the first analytic step: familiarization and event
segmentation. It does **not** assign analytic codes, construct themes, map the
data to JITAI, or derive system requirements.

The unit of interest is a participant account in which a planned or ongoing
task changes course and, where available, the participant describes what
happened afterward. An account is retained even when it is a negative or weak
case, but its evidential status is made explicit.

## Segmentation rules

1. Begin an event when the participant introduces a particular task or plan and
   a concrete change to it.
2. End it when an outcome, later disposition, or clear transition to general or
   hypothetical discussion is reached.
3. Keep a later resumption account separate when it concerns a different task.
4. Mark interviewer-constructed or strongly reframed examples rather than
   treating them as spontaneous critical incidents.
5. Separate experienced accounts from preferences about a hypothetical system.
6. Do not infer an unreported recovery, internal state, or causal relationship.

## Hypothetical-system boundary

For every interview, discussion following a prompt such as “if there were a
system” is retained as `hypothetical system discussion` at this stage. It is
not merged with the experienced event and cannot establish that a proposed
feature is acceptable, usable, or effective. Exact hypothetical segments will
be attached to relevant excerpts during the later coding step.

## Segmentation result

- 14 anonymized participants were reviewed.
- 17 records are retained because P08, P11, and P13 each require two distinct
  records for auditability.
- Only P03 clearly describes an external interruption during active execution.
- Several records concern non-start, self-initiated stopping, duration mismatch,
  or project-level delay rather than execution interruption.
- P08-B is marked as interviewer-shaped, and P14 is retained as a negative case.

These distinctions are descriptive corpus properties, not themes.
